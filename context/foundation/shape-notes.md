## Vision & Problem Statement

Active learners struggle with the significant time investment required to manually create high-quality flashcards. When preparing for exams or dense material, they spend hours copying, pasting, and formatting questions instead of studying, leading them to either waste valuable time or abandon spaced repetition entirely.

While existing spaced repetition software (like Anki or SuperMemo) focuses heavily on deck management and review algorithms, the true barrier to adoption is the creation flow. Providing a fast, AI-powered extraction pipeline turns raw text into high-quality cards instantly, removing the friction of deck creation and unlocking the power of active recall for learners.

- **Socrates 100x Scale Consideration**: If scale grows 100x, backend LLM API costs would scale linearly and become a primary bottleneck; the product would need to adopt caching of popular material, local client-side card processing, or user-supplied API keys to control runtime margins.

## User & Persona

### Primary Persona: Alex, the Exam Prep Learner

Alex is a university student or self-directed professional preparing for highly dense exams (e.g., medical, law, or technical certifications). Alex reaches for 10xCards when they are handed a new chapter, syllabus, or lecture notes and need to transform thousands of words of raw text into a study-ready review deck without spending days doing manual data entry.

## Access Control

The MVP uses a standard, secure multi-user model with a flat permission structure.

- **Authentication**: Users sign up and log in using an email and password to access their personal dashboard and store their generated decks in the cloud.
- **Roles & Permissions**:
  - **Standard User**: Flat user role. Each user can only view, create, edit, delete, and review their own flashcards and decks. No user can view or access decks belonging to another user.
  - **Unauthenticated State**: Anyone hitting the app's domain is redirected to a landing page with options to Log In or Sign Up. Gated routes (such as deck generation, card management, and study interfaces) are strictly protected and redirect to login if accessed without a session.

## Success Criteria

### Primary

- At least 75% of flashcards generated by the AI are accepted by the user with zero or minor manual edits.
- Active learners create at least 75% of their total cards in the system using the AI generation flow rather than typing them from scratch.

### Secondary

- Users complete at least 3 spaced repetition study sessions within their first week of registration, proving sustained engagement.

### Guardrails

- **Data Privacy**: Input texts pasted for AI generation are never cached long-term or used for model training.
- **Performance Floor**: AI card extraction completes in under 5 seconds for source texts under 1,000 words.

## Functional Requirements

- FR-001: User can sign up with an email and password. Priority: must-have
  > Socrates: Counter-argument considered: "friction barrier on day one." Resolution: kept; sign-up is required to securely bind cloud storage for user decks.
- FR-002: User can log in with their email and password. Priority: must-have
  > Socrates: Counter-argument considered: "session expiration disrupts flow." Resolution: kept; standard login is essential to restore the user session securely.

### Deck & Card Management

- FR-003: User can create a new named deck. Priority: must-have
  > Socrates: Counter-argument considered: "decks create artificial silos compared to tags." Resolution: kept; decks are a familiar industry-standard mental model that matches traditional learning.
- FR-004: User can view their list of decks. Priority: must-have
  > Socrates: Counter-argument considered: "simple list becomes cluttered or lacks progress." Resolution: kept; a simple flat list is sufficient for the first shippable release.
- FR-005: User can delete a deck and all its associated cards. Priority: must-have
  > Socrates: Counter-argument considered: "accidental data loss is catastrophic." Resolution: kept; standard browser/modal confirmation is sufficient to prevent accidents.
- FR-006: User can manually create a card (Front and Back text) inside a deck. Priority: must-have
  > Socrates: Counter-argument considered: "duplicates UI and dilutes AI focus." Resolution: kept; manual entry is a vital backup for cards AI misses or when users have specific custom notes.
- FR-007: User can edit and delete cards inside a deck. Priority: must-have
  > Socrates: Counter-argument considered: "breaks spaced repetition review continuity." Resolution: kept; being able to refine cards is standard behavior that must be present.

### AI Card Generation

- FR-008: User can paste raw text to trigger AI extraction of flashcards. Priority: must-have
  > Socrates: Counter-argument considered: "token overflow or card flood from pasting massive text." Resolution: kept; we will enforce a strict character/word limit (e.g., maximum 2,000 words / 10,000 characters) on the input textbox for the MVP.
- FR-009: User can view, edit, and select/deselect AI-generated draft cards before adding them to a deck. Priority: must-have
  > Socrates: Counter-argument considered: "decision fatigue from reviewing long lists of drafts." Resolution: kept; editing and filtering before committing is essential to maintaining high deck quality.

### Study & Spaced Repetition

- FR-010: User can start a review session for a deck (displaying only cards due for review). Priority: must-have
  > Socrates: Counter-argument considered: "stops eager students from studying ahead when zero cards are due." Resolution: kept; showing only due cards is the fundamental requirement of spaced repetition; studying ahead is an anti-pattern.
- FR-011: User can rate their recall of a card (e.g., Easy, Good, Hard) to automatically schedule its next review date. Priority: must-have
  > Socrates: Counter-argument considered: "three feedback levels are subjective." Resolution: kept; three levels match traditional Leitner or SM-2 algorithms perfectly.

### Export

- FR-012: User can export cards in Anki-compatible format (CSV/TXT). Priority: nice-to-have
  > Socrates: Counter-argument considered: "retention leak or brittle formatting issues." Resolution: kept; Anki compatibility makes 10xCards a powerful card creation tool that fits directly into existing power-user workflows.

## User Stories

### US-01: AI-Powered Card Generation from Text

- **Given** a logged-in user inside a deck
- **When** they paste a block of study notes and click "Generate Cards"
- **Then** they see a loading state followed by a list of draft cards showing a Question (front) and Answer (back) generated by the AI

#### Acceptance Criteria

- System extracts key facts from the text and formats them as concise Q&As.
- User can check/uncheck individual draft cards to select which ones are added.
- User can edit the text of any draft card before accepting.
- Empty or very short input text displays a validation error instead of calling the AI.

### US-02: User Registration and Login

- **Given** an unauthenticated visitor on the landing page
- **When** they fill out the registration form with a valid email and strong password
- **Then** their account is created, they are logged in automatically, and redirected to their dashboard

- **Given** a registered user on the login page
- **When** they submit their correct email and password
- **Then** they are logged in and redirected to their dashboard

#### Acceptance Criteria

- Sign-up validates email format and enforces basic password strength (minimum 8 characters).
- Form displays clear error feedback for incorrect login credentials or pre-existing email registrations.
- Gated routes (such as dashboards, deck details, and review screens) redirect back to the landing page if accessed by unauthenticated visitors.

### US-03: Deck Creation and Management

- **Given** a logged-in user on their dashboard
- **When** they click "Create New Deck" and enter a name
- **Then** a new deck is created and they see it in their deck list

- **Given** a logged-in user on their dashboard
- **When** they click "Delete Deck" on a specific deck and confirm the prompt
- **Then** the deck and all its associated cards are deleted permanently

#### Acceptance Criteria

- The dashboard lists all decks owned by the active user in a clean, flat list.
- Users must confirm deck deletion via a confirmation modal/dialog before the action is executed.
- The deck list displays card counts (total cards and cards due for review) for each deck.

### US-04: Manual Flashcard Creation and Management

- **Given** a logged-in user viewing a specific deck
- **When** they fill out the "New Card" form with front/back text and click save
- **Then** the new card is saved inside that deck and the list of cards updates

- **Given** a logged-in user viewing a specific card inside their deck
- **When** they edit the front/back text and save changes, or click delete
- **Then** the card's content is updated instantly, or the card is permanently deleted

#### Acceptance Criteria

- Empty front or back fields display a clear validation error and prevent card creation.
- Editing a card updates its text without modifying its current spaced-repetition scheduling parameters (repetition count, ease factor, review interval).
- Deleting a card removes it permanently from the database and active study queue.

### US-05: Spaced Repetition Study Session

- **Given** a logged-in user with a deck containing cards due for review
- **When** they start a study session
- **Then** they see the first due card's front side, with the back side and rating buttons hidden

- **Given** a user viewing the front of a due card
- **When** they click "Show Answer"
- **Then** they see the back of the card along with three recall rating buttons: "Easy", "Good", and "Hard"

- **Given** a user viewing both front and back of a card
- **When** they select their recall rating (Easy, Good, or Hard)
- **Then** the card's next review date is recalculated and the session immediately displays the next due card

#### Acceptance Criteria

- The study session displays only cards whose next-review timestamp is in the past (due for review).
- If zero cards are due, the deck page shows an encouraging "All caught up!" state and disables the "Start Review" button.
- Card-to-card transitions are fast (<100ms) with intuitive visual/interactive feedback.
- Completing all due cards in the session returns the user to the deck summary view.

## Business Logic

The application automatically extracts atomic, study-ready active recall questions from unstructured text and schedules their reviews using an adaptive spaced repetition calculation to guarantee optimal retention with minimal card creation effort.

### 1. AI Fact Extraction & Card Formulation (Generation Logic)

The core value-add domain logic extracts highly focused, standalone study-ready flashcards from any chunk of user-supplied text.

- **Input**: A raw, unstructured block of text (minimum 50 characters, maximum 10,000 characters).
- **Processing**: The system filters out non-declarative context, noise, and redundant fillers. It then breaks the key concepts down into atomic facts and reformulates each fact as a clear, single-concept Question (Front) and Answer (Back) matching academic active recall standards.
- **Output**: A flat array of 3 to 10 independent draft card objects containing front/back pairs.
- **Domain Constraint**: The generator must never invent or assume any facts not directly mentioned in the input text block.

### 2. Spaced Repetition Priority Calculation (Scheduling Logic)

The system calculates the exact next-review date for cards using an adaptive calculation modeled on the SuperMemo-2 (SM-2) or box-based Leitner scheduling system.

- **Input**: The card's current interval (days), current ease factor, repetition count, and the learner's rating of their recall (Easy, Good, Hard).
- **Processing**:
  - **Easy**: Extends the review interval significantly and slightly increases the ease factor.
  - **Good**: Multiplies the current interval by the ease factor.
  - **Hard**: Resets the interval (usually to 1 day or immediate re-review in current session) and slightly reduces the ease factor.
- **Output**: An updated next-due timestamp and status payload (stored in the database).
- **Domain Constraint**: Reviews must only display cards whose calculated next-due timestamp is in the past (due for review).

## Non-Functional Requirements

- **NFR-001 (Study Flow Latency)**: Card-to-card transitions during active study review sessions must render instantly (< 100ms) to ensure learners can maintain a fast, friction-free study flow.
- **NFR-002 (Broad Browser Compatibility)**: The web application must be fully functional and compatible across all major evergreen browsers, specifically including Safari, Firefox, and Chromium-based browsers (Chrome, Edge), ensuring students studying on non-Chromium devices have an identical experience.
- **NFR-003 (Strict Content Protection)**: For security and academic integrity, raw study materials pasted for AI generation must never be logged on the server, cached long-term, or submitted to public model-training pools.
- **NFR-004 (Input Safeguards)**: The deck creation text box must validate and reject text blocks exceeding 10,000 characters (roughly 2,000 words) before sending requests to the backend to guarantee card generation completes in under 10 seconds and limit token consumption.

## Non-Goals

- **No Native Mobile Applications**: The MVP is strictly 100% web-only to focus development speed and maintain single-codebase velocity (mobile layout will be responsive, but no iOS/Android builds will be compiled).
- **No Document Uploads**: We explicitly avoid building document parsing (PDF, DOCX, etc.) pipelines or OCR to stay within the 3-week timeline; text copy-paste is the sole ingestion model.
- **No Deck Sharing or Social Features**: All user data, decks, and review states are strictly private; collaborative decks, shared public repos, and social links are out of scope.
- **No Proprietary Algorithm Research**: We will not design or optimize a custom spaced-repetition algorithm from scratch; standard box-based Leitner or the classic SM-2 algorithm will be leveraged directly.
- **No Third-Party Platform Sync**: We will not build automatic integrations or live syncing with existing apps (like Notion, Quizlet, RemNote, or Obsidian).
